Validation against published examples

Each case is run live through the same code as the selector above. Partial pressures computed from composition use p = Pabs × mole fraction, as in the source. Field cases are independent worked or field examples. Rule cases restate a threshold that the tool takes from the same source, so they confirm the code, not the method.

Boundaries: your chart vs. the official catalogue

The chart you supplied is the version reproduced in Bellarby, Well Completion Design (2009). Its lines were measured by pixel analysis to ±1 px (about 0.02 decade) and then checked against Sumitomo's own Material Selection Guidelines catalogue, which prints the boundaries in MPa, and against nipponsteel.com.

Where the catalogue gives a value, the tool uses it. The main boundary correction is the CO₂ line: the official value is 0.02 MPa (2.90 psi), while the reproduction draws it at about 3.4 psia. The larger differences are in the temperature limits of the nickel alloys, listed below.

Limits of this method

Nippon Steel describes the chart as a general guideline based on CO₂, H₂S and temperature only. Final selection also depends on in-situ pH, chloride content, elemental sulfur and the full well life. This tool does not assess chloride, pH or elemental sulfur.

Partial pressure should be taken at the condition that gives the highest value, usually the bubble point for oil wells. At high pressure ISO 15156 recognises fugacity as more accurate than partial pressure. The composition tab computes Peng-Robinson fugacity coefficients, with the balance gas taken as methane, and can plot the fugacities instead.

The ISO 15156-2 temperature exemptions for API grades depend on the lowest temperature the pipe sees over its life, not the maximum. The tool takes the static geothermal profile as that minimum. Cool-down from injection or gas expansion is not modelled; enter a lower surface temperature to cover it.

Points within 0.1 decade of a boundary are flagged. Treat both neighbouring zones as candidates there.

Sources

    Grade names are Nippon Steel (formerly Sumitomo) SM-series designations and API 5CT grades. Temperature limits are from the official Sumitomo catalogue and Nippon Steel data sheets. ISO 15156-3 limits are transcribed from Annex A and should be checked against a licensed copy of the standard. This tool supports screening and does not replace a materials engineer's qualification.
